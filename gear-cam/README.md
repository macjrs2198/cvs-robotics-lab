# CVS Gear Cutter

This static app adds gear drawing and simple 2D router profile cutting to the CVS Robotics Lab. It accepts the six inputs from `GearBuilder_Fixed.xlsm` (or manual entries), rebuilds both outlines without running Excel macros, and exports a closed DXF drawing. It also accepts compatible closed DXF profiles for a GRBL cut job.

DXF export writes an AutoCAD 2018 drawing in millimeters by default, with standard drawing tables and objects, closed 2D `POLYLINE` contours on layer 0, and true `CIRCLE` entities for round holes. To use the drawing as editable geometry in Autodesk Inventor, create a Part, start a 2D Sketch, then choose Sketch → Insert → ACAD and select the exported profiles. The DXF contains the drawing contours, while the separate `.nc` download contains the compensated cutter path. The exporter can also write inches when called with `unit: 'in'`; the app's download uses millimeters.

## Workbook geometry

The source workbook uses pinion and gear tooth counts, pressure angle, center distance in inches, and two dedendum safety factors. `gear.js` reproduces its 40-point-per-tooth outlines and converts them to millimeters internally. The provided 30-tooth saved trace is used as a regression fixture. The original macro's `li` text is a LibreCAD drawing script, not machine G-code.

The two tooth counts and center distance determine tooth size: diametral pitch is `(pinion teeth + gear teeth) / (2 × center distance in inches)`. The default input mode holds center distance fixed like the workbook; the **Keep tooth size** checkbox instead holds diametral pitch fixed and updates center distance as tooth counts change. The app shows both pitch diameters, shared tooth spacing, and gear ratio while students edit. The workbook's pinion addendum formula supports 4–35 pinion teeth; the drawing and CAM vertex limit supports up to 150 gear teeth.

Before either gear is offered for export, the generator checks tip width, open spaces between roots, mating tip/root clearance, usable involute contact, continuous tooth contact, and the app's coordinate range. The original workbook formula crosses the pinion tip at 8 teeth with a 30-tooth mate and 20° pressure angle; this pair is now blocked, while 9 teeth passes those geometric checks. A fixed minimum tooth count would not work across pressure angles and gear pairs. If the root circle lies outside the base circle, the generated involute now starts at the root and follows a sampled root arc instead of dipping back toward the base circle. The supplied 10/30 workbook trace remains unchanged.

These checks establish a usable outline and basic mating geometry, not load strength or an exact manufactured fit. The workbook tooth thicknesses have zero nominal backlash; a teacher should still check material clearance, cutter access, and a test fit before relying on cut gears.

## Router workflow

1. Choose **Gear generator** or **DXF drawing**. In Gear generator, load the workbook or enter gear values and select a gear outline. A centered bore, if requested, becomes its own inside profile. In DXF drawing, open your own flat part drawing; its shape does not need to be a gear. Switching modes keeps the loaded outlines, chosen cut sides, tabs, and cut order for each source during the session. Router settings are shared. Check the origin and cut sides again after switching.
2. Choose what should remain for each profile and move profiles up or down into the desired cut order. **Keep the gear** places the bit outside its outline; **Make the center hole** places the bit inside its circle. Green fill shows the finished part that remains, pale orange fill shows removed openings, and the green line is the intended finished edge. As soon as the bit diameter and its units are entered, the darker orange cutter-width band and dashed bit center update immediately when the cut result changes; feeds, depths, and safety confirmations are not needed for this 2D check. Overlapping passes in waste are allowed when the full cutter clears the finished edges. Add holding tabs to outer cutouts, then inspect their positions in the completed preview.
3. Enter measured stock thickness and the number printed on the installed bit, then explicitly choose millimeters or inches for that bit. The app converts the bit to millimeters, displays both units, and requires the operator to confirm that the entry matches the installed tool. Enter either a pass count or depth per pass in millimeters, feed rates, safe Z, spindle RPM, and the chosen work origin. The other pass value updates automatically. Download the `.nc` file and inspect its paths and Z moves in Universal G-code Sender before running an air cut.

The DXF reader accepts flat, closed `LWPOLYLINE`, legacy 2D `POLYLINE`, and `CIRCLE` entities, including polyline bulge arcs. Connected `LINE`, `ARC`, `SPLINE`, and open 2D polyline segments on the same layer join into closed contours, even when their order and directions differ. Curves are sampled in millimeters with a maximum 0.02 mm chord deviation; joining only matches endpoints within 0.0001 mm. Two curved segments can form a closed loop. Real gaps, branches, nonplanar entities, and unsupported geometry are still rejected with an explanation. Export ellipses and unsupported spline forms as 2D polylines and explode blocks in CAD before importing them. A failed import clears the active drawing and disables exports.

Spline import supports clamped B-splines of degrees 1–8 with positive rational weights, subject to the existing vertex limits and a 6,000-control refinement limit. `spline.js` inserts internal knots to produce exact rational Bezier pieces, then uses homogeneous de Casteljau subdivision until the full projected control hull is within 0.02 mm of its chord. This preserves bends that a midpoint-only check can miss. Periodic, unclamped, fit-only, discontinuous, nonplanar, and nonpositive-weight representations report a conversion instruction. Spline metadata follows the [Autodesk DXF reference](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/AutoCAD-DXF/files/GUID-E1F884F8-AA90-4864-A215-3182D47A9C74.htm); the knot insertion method is described in [MIT's B-spline algorithms reference](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node18.html).

**Skip text and dimensions** explicitly omits `TEXT`, `MTEXT`, `DIMENSION`, `LEADER`, and `MULTILEADER` annotations and lists the skipped types and counts. It never skips unsupported cutting geometry. The reader uses inch or millimeter `$INSUNITS`; unitless drawings require an explicit unit choice. A job can contain up to 32 profiles. This version makes closed profile cuts only: no pockets, open-path engraving, drilling cycles, or separate roughing/finishing passes. Tabs are available on outside cuts.

The CAM engine checks the swept bit against the finished edges, rather than requiring separate nonoverlapping kerfs. Source contours must remain simple and separate, but compensated paths can overlap, touch, or retrace in waste. A gap between two retained edges needs one full bit diameter, with a 0.000001 mm numerical clearance tolerance. For example, a 3 mm cutter passes a 3 mm gap and fails a 2.99 mm gap. Zero-length compensated moves are removed; an exact-width inside slot can reduce to an out-and-back centerline. Point-collapse cuts remain outside this profile workflow. Every generated segment still needs a full cutter radius of clearance from its own contour and all neighboring finished edges. Preview and G-code use the same paths and checks.

G-code is in millimeters (`G21`) and uses absolute work coordinates (`G90`, `G54`) with Z0 on the stock top. Each file header records the cutter diameter in both millimeters and inches. The app calculates the tool-center path; GRBL does not provide cutter compensation for this job. The classroom profile workflow accepts cutters at least 0.5 mm in diameter. Machine settings are intentionally required rather than assumed. All cuts plunge at the start of each depth pass, so the bit must be able to plunge in the selected material. A full-depth cut needs a secure hold-down plan for every loose piece. The output cannot verify a real machine's travel, clamps, tool, spindle wiring, or material setup.
